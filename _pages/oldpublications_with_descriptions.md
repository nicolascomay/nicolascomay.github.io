# 2025
## <a href="https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/full/10.1098/rsos.231805" target="_blank">Iconic versus working memory metacognition to evaluate the richness of perception: a registered report<i class="fa fa-external-link" style="font-size:0.75em; margin-left:6px;"></i></a>

In this registered report we compared metacognition in iconic memory and working memory. Although iconic memory showed higher capacity, metacognitive sensitivity was lower than in working memory—even when task performance was matched. These results suggest that iconic memory supports identification with information that is less consciously accessible than that held in working memory.

**Comay, N. A.**, Solovey, G., & Barttfeld, P. (2025). Iconic versus working memory metacognition to evaluate the richness of perception: a registered report. _Royal Society Open Science, 12_(11), 231805. <a href="/files/2025_registered_rep_RSOS.pdf" target="_blank"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf" style="margin-left:6px;"></i></a> <a href="https://osf.io/nc5hw/overview" target="_blank"><i class="fa-solid fa-database" style="margin-left:6px;"></i></a>

##  <a href="https://doi.org/10.1037/xlm0001532" target="_blank">Decisions are based on less information than metacognitive judgments in multialternative contexts<i class="fa fa-external-link" style="font-size:0.75em; margin-left:6px;"></i></a>
Often, dissociations between metacognition and decision accuracy take the form of metacognitive inefficiency—confidence being less informative than it could be. Conversely, in this study we found that in multialternative choices, the metacognitive system may be more robust than the decision system.

**Comay, N. A.**, Solovey, G., & Barttfeld, P. (2025). ##  <a href="https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1618-3169/a000636" target="_blank">Metacognitive sensitivity on the Iowa Gambling Task reveals awareness as a necessary condition for advantageous performance<i class="fa fa-external-link" style="font-size:0.75em; margin-left:6px;"></i></a>
The somatic marker hypothesis (SMH) proposes that bodily affective signals guide our decision-making advantageously independently of our awareness of which alternatives are better. Here, we used metacognitive sensitivity measures to quantify participants’ awareness during the Iowa Gambling Task and found that metacognition predicted performance across virtually all blocks of the task. These results suggest that awareness may play a more central role in advantageous decision-making than previously assumed by the SMH.

Zapata, J. M.<sup>+</sup>, **Comay, N. A.**<sup>+</sup>, Taricco, G., Barttfeld, P., Solovey, G., Saal, A., & Ahumada, J. V. (2024). Metacognitive sensitivity on the Iowa Gambling Task reveals awareness as a necessary condition for advantageous performance. _Experimental Psychology, 71_(6), 343–351. <sup>+</sup>joint first authors. <a href="/files/2025_igt_metacog_experimentalpsych.pdf" target="_blank"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf" style="margin-left:6px;"></i></a><a href="https://github.com/JulietaZapata/IGTandMetacognition" target="_blank"><i class="fa-solid fa-database" style="margin-left:6px;"></i></a>

## <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2023.105377" target="_blank">The presence of irrelevant alternatives paradoxically increases confidence in perceptual decisions<i class="fa fa-external-link" style="font-size:0.75em; margin-left:6px;"></i></a>
The Bayesian confidence hypothesis states that confidence reflects the probability of being correct. If so, the presence of irrelevant, clearly incorrect alternatives should not affect confidence, as these options do not modify this probability. Contrary to normative predictions, we found that the presence of this kind of alternatives increased confidence, an effect which we capture with an alternative Bayesian model. These results highlight the need for more complex tasks involving multiple alternatives in order to achieve a thorough understanding of confidence computations.

**Comay, N. A.**, Della Bella, G., Lamberti, P., Sigman, M., Solovey, G., & Barttfeld, P. (2023).
